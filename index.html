How I work

  1. Start with the story ➜

    Who needs to think, feel or act differently, and what would make them? Every project here started with that question and people at the centre

  2. Come at it sideways ✎

    A sticker dispenser for a talk about language. New icons systme, a fun 80s brand. The unexpected route is often the one people remember.

  3. Make the thing ✄

    I’d rather prototype than present a deck about a prototype. I sketch, build and test early, and share the work while it’s still rough.

  4. Built to last ➷

    Brand manuals, templates and InDesign styles, so the work holds up after launch day when someone else has to use it.

Cover Letter

For the Designer role at Tilt

Hi Tilt team,

I’d love to be considered for the Designer role at Tilt. I’m a Brighton-based multidisciplinary designer with over a decade of experience across brand identity, editorial and campaign design, working end-to-end from concept through to production-ready delivery.

My client work spans community-led organisations to national institutions, including Tate Britain, Nike, Hachette, Samsung, Stonewall and the Barbican.

A few recent projects give a good sense of the range I can bring. For Dream Street Records, a queer record store in the North Laine, I led brand strategy and art direction end-to-end: identity, Shopify storefront, merch, campaign assets and a system flexible enough to hold the store’s whole personality across every touchpoint. For Free Word’s “Finding Power” season of events, I anchored a concept-led identity in protest visuals and DIY print histories, then adapted it fast across banners, social, editorial, print and wall graphics. For the Inclusive Mosque Initiative, I ran co-design workshops and stakeholder facilitation to build a visual system with real tonal range: soft and community-facing one moment, funder-ready the next.

I’m confident across Adobe Creative Suite (I use InDesign daily) and Figma. I’ve been actively using AI tools in my process over the past year: they’ve helped me bridge gaps in coding and web design, and build systems that speed up production and project management, but they’ve never substituted for the thinking.

I also lecture in graphic design at the University of Brighton, which keeps me close to how design education and practice are shifting, and means I’m used to giving and taking feedback constructively, something your ad clearly values.

I’d love the chance to bring both the craft and the collaborative energy to your studio. My portfolio is attached, and I’m happy to talk through the process behind any of it.

All the best,
Soofiya

Where I think design is heading

01

AI makes more options.

When design can seem to be cheap to generate, being able to say “no, not that, and here’s why” becomes more valuable an that whats we offer as designers.

02

Designers can build now.

I love that I can prototype working tools in an afternoon. Its exciting to be able ship something and get there first.

03

Understand the breif

Clear thinking about audience and intent is what gets good output from an AI model, its just a tool

04

It is still human.

Generated work gets you only so far. A judgement call on tone is still core to our job.
